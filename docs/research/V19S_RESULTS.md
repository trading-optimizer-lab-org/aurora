## Summary

- **Trading COMBO19's members in one account instead of averaging them multiplies the return.** At v19's own sizing
  (0.5% risk to the stop, 20% cap, gross 1x, $100k) the joint book makes 81% a year on fixed capital (114% CAGR
  compounded), drawdown -25%, monthly Sortino 7.3, against COMBO19's 16% and -3.9%: the average held each family at
  about a quarter.
- **The pre-registered choice is 1% risk per trade for every account size** ($100k: cap 100%, gross 1x; $25k and
  $250k: cap 20%, gross 1.5x): 159% a year on fixed capital (311% CAGR), drawdown -45%, worst day -37%, Sortino 8.0;
  in the bootstrap a 50% drawdown within ten years has a 22% chance, a 75% one about 0% (the rule: at most 1%).
- **Above 1% the risk of ruin climbs fast**: at 1.5% a 75% drawdown has a 7% chance, at 2% 73%; from 3% some books
  lose the whole account in 2018-2026 (a day losing more than the account).
- **The worst days are squeezes of DS1's daily run-up shorts** (2020-10-19: DS1's book -15.9% in a day, the joint book
  -18.5% at 0.5% risk and -37% at 1%).
- **Fragile to costs**: at the high costs (half-spreads x2, borrow fees x3) the 1% book makes 45% CAGR with an 84%
  drawdown and a 26% chance of a 75% drawdown; at 0.5% risk 25% CAGR, drawdown -56%.
- **Caveats.** The compounded CAGRs are not reachable: the liquidity caps are computed on the starting account, so as
  equity grows the positions would hit the 2%-of-volume limit (the yearly return on fixed capital is the better
  guide); the picks and their period were seen before; borrow availability for larger positions is not measured; the
  bootstrap cannot produce a squeeze larger than the worst in the sample.

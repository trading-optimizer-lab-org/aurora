## Verdict by the pre-registered reading: DATA (extended-session dependence)

Two of the "data" conditions hold:

- **The minute edge lives only in premarket-limit exits.** Selling at the regular open (d1o) loses in every year
  but 2020, net of costs: c_1555 d1o trimmed PF 0.43-0.71 in 2016-2019 and 2021-2026 (1.33 in 2020), median trade
  -1.0% to -2.1%. The same entry sold by the premarket limit (d1pm) has trimmed PF 1.0-1.3 from 2018 and medians of
  +1.2% to +1.5% from 2021. Selling at 10:00, at the close or the second close loses everywhere but 2020.
- **Premarket coverage differs.** The share of CRASH events with no premarket bar next day: 19.0% and 14.5% in
  2016-2017, 11.7% and 9.7% in 2018-2019, 27.5% in 2020, then 1.1-4.7% from 2021. The median count of premarket
  bars: 6-16 in 2016-2019 against 30-68 from 2021; after-hours bars follow the same step.

## What the three looks say

- **Daily panel, gross**: buying a -20% close and selling the next open has a trimmed PF above 1 in every year,
  2016-2017 included (2.68, 1.71); the overnight bounce exists in every year before costs. Held to the next close it
  is gone (trimmed PF 0.75-0.95 except 2016 and 2020).
- **Minute bars, net**: entered at 15:55 at the minute price and sold at the regular open, the bounce does not pay
  for the spread and costs in any year but 2020.
- **Where the profit is**: the premarket limit (close + 3%, else the open) captures the early part of the bounce.
  It needs premarket trades through the limit; those were sparse before 2021. The c_1555 d1pm cells are flat in
  2016-2019 (trimmed PF 0.88-1.08) and positive from 2020 (1.16-1.77, 0.92 in 2026); 2018-2022's PF of 1.53 in v52
  came mostly from 2020. c_ah0 d1pm (after-hours limit at the close) has a trimmed PF above 1 in every year (1.08-2.19), 2016-2017
  included (trimmed 1.74, 1.89), on half the trades because the after-hours limit fills only about half the time.
- Daily and minute closes agree (median gap 0.2-0.4%, over 2% in 3-13% of events) and so do the opens (over 2% in
  under 1%): the bars are consistent with the daily panel; the difference is how much trading the extended sessions
  hold, not a broken file.

## Reading

The question "why not in 2016-2017" has a plain answer: the strategy makes its money selling into premarket trades,
and before 2021 there were few of them; from 2021 premarket and after-hours trading in these names grew about five
times. That is a real market change, but it makes the result rest on the backtest's extended-session fill rule
(a limit fills when any minute bar trades through it), which was already flagged as optimistic. Thin premarket
prints, odd lots and one-share trades can satisfy that rule without a real order filling. Every v51-v53 result that
leans on premarket or after-hours limits (CRASH-long d1pm, the c_ah entries) carries that risk, and the 2018 on
numbers should be read with it. The test that would settle it is fills against real quotes or a fill rule that
needs volume at the limit, not more years of bars.
